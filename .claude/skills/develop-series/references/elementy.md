# Recurring elements

What `develop-series` reads when the conversation reaches the protagonist and their look: the
running list, the rows each entry has to close, and the facts that sit between entries.
Nothing downstream invents appearance; the image model receives this prose and nothing else.

## The running list

Any answer can introduce a recurring element: a character of the cast, a prop or a setting.
Put it on the list the moment it appears, and treat the list, not this page, as what must be
described before the session ends.

- **Only the main character is on it from the start.** Everything else is there because the
  user put it there. One series has three recurring characters and no props, another a single
  character and four; nothing belongs on the list because you expected it, and nothing stays
  off it because you did not.
- **Naming is not describing.** "Nosi ten sam strój" and "ma swój przedmiot" are inventory,
  not appearance, and a renderer given only those draws something new each time. So an answer
  that adds an entry makes that entry the next question: two exchanges, not one.
- **Detail follows screen time, not kind.** A prop the story leans on earns as much detail as
  a character, because it will be in as many frames.
- **The boundary is recurrence, not subject matter.** Would it have to look the same in
  episode seven? Then it is on the list; if not, it is not yours to describe.

## The rows an entry closes

Work down the list until every entry is something a renderer could act on. Photographs settle
a character's face and body and nothing else, so with photographs skip that one row.

- **Figure proportions**, every character: head-to-body ratio and how far the figure departs
  from nature. Ask it on purpose, because people describe colour first and never get to it,
  and ask it apart from the aspect ratio; when the two get confused,
  [docs/slownik.md](../../../../docs/slownik.md#proporcje-i-proporcje) has the pair.
- **Face and body**, every character: age read, build, skin tone, hair colour, hair shape and
  length, eyes, distinguishing features. An age and a role together are a casting note, not
  an appearance.
- **Recurring wardrobe**, every character: the exact outfit that returns every episode, its
  garments, cut and colours. In flat or stylised 2D the outfit carries recognition more
  reliably than the face, so this row is never optional.
- **Each prop.** First ask whether any prop recurs at all; many series have none, and
  inventing one is not your job. For each that does: what it is, its size against the
  character, shape, material, colour, wear, and whatever makes it *that* one rather than a
  generic one.
- **Each setting:** what is fixed in it, its palette, its light, and the furnishings that may
  not move between episodes.

## What holds across entries

Some facts live between two entries and therefore sit on neither. Make one pass over the
finished list asking what holds across it rather than within one entry:

- **Scale.** Two independent ratios do not fix a relation. "Her head is a third of her height"
  and "his is a quarter of his" agree with "he is a head taller" only if their heads are the
  same size, which nobody said, and the character stage, drawing each figure separately, has
  no reason to assume. State the relation, not just the two ratios.
- **Shared construction:** what is drawn the same way on everyone, such as how faces are
  treated and how hands and joints are handled. Said once for the cast, it cannot contradict
  itself by the third entry.
- **Palette.** Check each entry's colours against the Style Bible's palette: colours invented
  per entry are how a cast ends up not looking like a cast.

## Before the hand-off

Re-read the list. An entry named but not described is an unasked question, and so is a
relation between entries that nobody stated. If the user truly cannot answer one yet, that is
allowed: say plainly that the character stage will then invent it, differently on every run.
